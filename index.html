Subscribers · churn, ARPU, lifetime value

Under 1% a month.
Nine-plus years a subscriber.

US postpaid churn runs 0.85–0.99% a month, which means the average postpaid subscriber stays around 100–118 months. That lifetime is why carriers can afford to pay hundreds of dollars to win a subscriber, and why a few basis points of churn are worth hundreds of millions.

01

The 110-month truth

Q2 2026 monthly churn → average lifetime

Average lifetime = 1 ÷ monthly churn. At 0.86% that is 116 months. This is a long-run average for a stable base, not a promise for any one subscriber. Check what each figure measures: T-Mobile's 0.85% phone churn was said by its CFO on the call; its reported metric is account churn, 0.99%.

What comes next

auctions and the pipeline
Law · Jul 4, 2025

One Big Beautiful Bill Act

Restored the FCC's auction authority through Sep 30, 2034 and set a pipeline of at least 800 MHz: 500 MHz from federal users, 300 MHz from the FCC, including at least 100 MHz of upper C-band within about two years.

Auction · by July 2027

Upper C-band

160 MHz at 3.98–4.14 GHz in 3,248 licences. Order adopted Jul 22, 2026; the auction must close by July 2027; service starts December 2030 in the top 75 markets. Mid-band like this is the capacity layer of 5G.

Pending · FCC

SpaceX + Grain 800 MHz

Needs FCC approval. The SpaceX–EchoStar transaction was approved on May 12, 2026, with spectrum transferred May 22 and final closing targeted for Nov 30, 2027, when EchoStar is due to hold about 3% of SpaceX.

Is SpaceX becoming a fourth carrier?

the case each way

Yes, eventually

  • 01It owns terrestrial-grade spectrum now: AWS-4/H-block and AWS-3 from EchoStar, and (pending) 800 MHz low-band from Grain.
  • 02It said so. SpaceX described the Grain spectrum as its ticket to becoming a "major mobile carrier"; Gwynne Shotwell said the EchoStar spectrum has terrestrial components and SpaceX will build ground infrastructure.
  • 03It has the cash and the currency: $2.21T market value, stock it can pay sellers with, and a broadband base of 12M.

Not soon

  • 01Satellite capacity per phone is tiny. T-Mobile says T-Satellite usage is "a lot less" than expected, about 0.0002% of its network traffic (May 2026).
  • 02No date. SpaceX has given no launch date for full US mobile service; next-generation satellites come in 2027. The EchoStar deal's final closing is Nov 30, 2027.
  • 03The carriers shut the door on wholesale. Verizon: "There is no backdoor to our MVNOs in any structure." T-Mobile ruled out an MVNO with SpaceX, and the three formed their own satellite JV.

03

Keeping SpaceX out of mobile

the defence, sized with the same metrics

The licences can't be taken back. SpaceX owns EchoStar's AWS-4, H-block and AWS-3 spectrum and has agreed to buy Grain's 800 MHz. What the Big Three can do is make a fourth retail network uneconomic, so SpaceX stays what it is to T-Mobile today: a supplier of satellite coverage, not a carrier selling phones.

What SpaceX has

  • 01Spectrum: ≈$19.6B of EchoStar licences (AWS-4/H-block and AWS-3), plus up to 14 MHz paired of 800 MHz from Grain (≈$8B reported, pending FCC approval).
  • 02Satellites: 650+ direct-to-cell satellites reaching ≈7.4M devices a month through ≈30 carrier partners. Next-generation satellites come in 2027.
  • 03Money: a $2.21T market value, and stock it can pay sellers with (up to $11B of the EchoStar price).
  • 04A channel: ≈12M Starlink broadband households worldwide, many of them rural.

What it still needs

  • 01Towers. Indoor and city service needs a ground network. Analyst Tim Farrar calls the 800 MHz "a very limited amount of spectrum"; SpaceX says it will build a terrestrial network but has given no date.
  • 02Capacity. Satellite traffic is ≈0.0002% of T-Mobile's network. Satellites cover gaps; they don't carry the 159.3 trillion MB a year Americans use.
  • 03Subscribers who leave. Postpaid churn is 0.85–0.99% a month: the average subscriber stays ≈100–118 months.
  • 04A shortcut. Renting a carrier's network (an MVNO) is how cable added 854K lines in Q2. Verizon and T-Mobile have both said no to SpaceX.
The precedent is EchoStar. It held 600 MHz, AWS-4, H-block, AWS-3 and 3.45 GHz licences and ran its own brand. It ended with Boost at 7.38M subscribers (down 118K in Q2), riding AT&T's radio network, and sold its spectrum to AT&T and SpaceX. Spectrum is not a network, and a network is not subscribers.

Seven moves

Starve the pool

A new carrier can only grow from subscribers who are already leaving someone else. Shrink that pool with churn, and cut the share SpaceX can win with coverage, price and a closed wholesale door.

From 0.86% (AT&T phones), 0.92% (Verizon phones), 0.99% (T-Mobile accounts).
Nobody knows this number. It is what coverage, price and wholesale access decide.
Do it alone, not together. Each move above is something each carrier decides for itself. An agreement among AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile to refuse SpaceX wholesale access, roaming or tower space would be a group boycott under US antitrust law. What works for any of them

the mechanics of telco revenue
Keep

Churn is the cheapest revenue

At 110+ month lifetimes, every subscriber kept is worth years of revenue with no acquisition cost. A 0.05-point cut in monthly churn is worth $300–420M a year of run-rate revenue at any of the three (Lever Lab). Better CRM, proactive outage credits and bundle depth move it.

Converge

Sell the home to the phone

Fiber and fixed wireless are where all three add subscribers fastest. A household with both mobile and home internet from one carrier is harder to lose. AT&T has 38.6M fiber locations to fill; Verizon 30M after Frontier; T-Mobile is building through JVs.

Price

Pricing power, used carefully

1% on the postpaid base is worth $0.5–0.7B a year at each carrier. It only works if churn holds: the Lab's last slider shows how fast a price rise is eaten by subscribers leaving. Verizon's ARPA fell 1.4% in Q2; T-Mobile's rose 2%.

Connect

More things per account

Watches, tablets, cars, trackers and business IoT add lines to existing accounts at little cost. It's the reason carriers moved to ARPA: the account is the subscriber, the line count is the upside.

Spectrum

Turn licences into products

AT&T's new 3.45 GHz and 600 MHz can carry fixed wireless (Internet Air) where fiber isn't built. Spectrum that sits idle earns nothing; T-Mobile sold 800 MHz it didn't need for $2.9B.

Satellite

A feature, not a revenue line (yet)

Even 1% of a carrier's base paying $10 a month is only $40–90M a year. Satellite earns its keep by removing a reason to switch, not by billing for it. The carriers' joint venture is designed to stop SpaceX owning that feature.
